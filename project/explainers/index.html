01When a canvasser knocks
They must ask your permission before they record anything about you. You can say no. If you say no, nothing is stored, and there is no list of people who said no.Voters0:20 02Your answer to a poll
A poll asks for a choice and nothing else: no name, number or ID. Your answer is counted with others and is not linked to you. You can pick “Prefer not to say”.Voters0:20 03How seats are won
Some seats are won in wards, the rest from the party vote. Schedule 1 of the Municipal Structures Act turns votes into seats. The calculator shows the working, with the law cited.Voters0:31 04Seven roles, each sees only its own ground
A ward lead sees a ward. A canvasser sees a voting district. The refusal is made by the database rules, not only by the screen.Parties0:13 05One view of the campaign
People recorded with consent, doors worked, incidents open. Counts only. Nothing on it names a voter.Parties0:10 06Capture with consent
The canvasser types the person’s name back before the record button works, so a mis-tap cannot create consent. A refusal stores nothing.Parties0:12 07Offline in a dead zone
Work is kept on the phone and sent when signal returns. The screen shows what is still waiting. Clearing the browser’s data removes anything not yet sent, and the app says so.Parties0:15 08Funding line and the 30-day clock
A donor’s payments are added up against the R200,000 disclosure line (Government Gazette 53182, under court challenge). A person’s request about their own data is tracked against a 30-day target.Parties0:14 09Sentiment polls: what they can and cannot tell you
Answers come from people who chose to reply to a link. They are not a random sample, so there is no margin of error and no forecast. Groups under five answers are hidden.Parties0:30 10The scenario runner: your assumptions, run many times
You set the swing and how unsure you are. The software repeats the seat calculation across that range and shows which assumption matters most. It is not a prediction.Parties0:30
